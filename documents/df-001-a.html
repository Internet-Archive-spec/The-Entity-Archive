Reporting Agency: Bureau of Entity Observation (Preliminary Response Division)

Location: Redacted – Central Great Plains, United States

Date: ██/██/19██

Incident Summary

At approximately 16:42 local time, the National Severe Storm Warning Network began issuing emergency alerts following the rapid formation of an exceptionally violent supercell. Initial forecasts predicted a single EF4 tornado.

Within eleven minutes, radar identified two large tornadoes rotating around one another while maintaining a fixed distance. Meteorologists were unable to explain how both funnels maintained identical speed, structure, and movement without merging.

The event was initially classified as an unprecedented meteorological anomaly.

Field Observations

At approximately 17:08, emergency responders reported repeated lightning strikes occurring beneath the cloud base despite no measurable electrical buildup capable of producing them.

Officer Daniel Mercer submitted the first report mentioning "an enormous wolf running inside the storm."

The statement was dismissed due to exhaustion and severe psychological stress.

Thirty-four additional witnesses later provided nearly identical descriptions despite having no contact with one another.

Aerial Surveillance

At 17:26, a state police helicopter attempted to observe the storm from the southeast.

Video transmission lasted forty-seven seconds before being interrupted by repeated electrical interference.

The final recovered frame depicts what appears to be a massive quadrupedal silhouette extending through the cloud structure.

Image analysis remains inconclusive.

Bureau Assessment

Following comparison of radar archives, satellite imagery, eyewitness testimony, and recovered aerial footage, investigators concluded the event could not be explained through known atmospheric processes.

The phenomenon received the provisional designation: BEO-001

Codename: GigaCell

Closing Statement

"Natural disasters do not move with purpose.

Storms do not possess anatomy.

If what we've documented is real...

then this was never a tornado."

— Senior Investigator Helen Cross
